Your First Trip to Georgia

April 16, 2026
Posted by George Smith / 3 Comments

Georgia sits where Europe meets Asia, between the Black Sea and the peaks of the Greater Caucasus, and it is still one of the easiest countries in the region to visit. Travelers from the US and the EU need no visa for stays of up to a year, Tbilisi is a short flight from most European hubs, and the food alone is worth the trip. Give it a week: three days in Tbilisi, two in the mountains around Kazbegi and two in the wine country of Kakheti.

Start in the old town of Tbilisi, where carved wooden balconies hang over narrow streets and the sulfur baths have been open for centuries. Take the cable car up to Narikala fortress in the late afternoon, when the light is soft and the city spreads out along the river below you. Evenings are for long dinners: try khachapuri, the famous cheese bread, a plate of khinkali dumplings and a glass of amber wine made in clay jars the old way. From Tbilisi, the Georgian Military Highway climbs north for about three hours to Stepantsminda, the village most people still call Kazbegi. The drive is part of the trip, with stops at the Ananuri fortress above a turquoise reservoir and the viewpoints near Gudauri. In Kazbegi, the walk up to the Gergeti Trinity Church takes around ninety minutes and ends with a view of Mount Kazbek, more than 5,000 meters high, rising straight behind the fourteenth-century church. If the weather is clear, stay the night: sunrise over the glacier is the moment most of our travelers remember best. Guesthouses in the village are simple and friendly, and many hosts will cook dinner for you with vegetables from their own gardens and bread baked that morning.

The best time to go is from late May to early October. The mountain roads are open, the hiking trails are dry and the grape harvest in Kakheti starts in September. Winter is quieter and colder, but Tbilisi stays lively and the ski slopes at Gudauri are some of the best value in Europe. Pack layers either way, since the mountains can be cool even in August.

Getting around is easier than many people expect. Minibuses connect most towns for a few dollars, but a private driver lets you stop wherever the view demands it and often costs less than a rental car with full insurance. Our Caucasus packages include a driver and an English-speaking guide for the mountain days, with free time in Tbilisi to explore at your own pace. Prices start at $159 per person, and we can add extra nights, a wine tasting or a day trip to the cave city of Vardzia. Ask our team for a quote and we will plan the route around your dates.
